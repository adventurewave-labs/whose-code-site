What the numbers actually say

Ancuria shows what a confession looks like. 37 commits are classified assisted from direct Co-Authored-By: Claude trailers the agent left behind, and the velocity signal flagged 152 commits on days that broke the author's own rhythm. Those are honest fingerprints, counted, not hidden.

Turbo-rig shows the hole the industry is standing over. 18,282 commits and effectively no machine evidence at all, because its history was written before agent tools started signing their work. The scanner says unknown on all of it, and unknown is the truthful answer: a repository without trail artifacts cannot prove anything about who wrote it or who approved it. That is not a defect in the scanner. That is the multi-billion-dollar problem the whole category is about to collide with.

Zero false positives, stated precisely. No commit was classified assisted or machine at deciding confidence without a direct confession. That proves indirect signals never decided a class on our code. It does not claim every classification is accurate, and the report itself says so.

The method

Version 0.2, stamped on every report: commit trailers naming known AI identities, bot accounts, review artifacts, and velocity outliers. Classes are assisted / machine / unknown. There is no "human" class by design: a commit message can prove machine involvement, never human authorship. Every record is cross-checked against git's own authoritative history, and anything the scanner cannot read fails the scan instead of guessing.

The scan itself was gated the same way we gate everything: an independent reviewer, 14 rounds on the scanner, 12 on the validation receipt, every finding fixed with a regression test before a human merged.

What this is for

Gartner says 70% of enterprises will abandon vendor-built agentic AI by 2028. The reason nobody puts in the forecast: buyers may not own what they paid for, and they cannot prove the human part either. The answer is evidence, produced while the work happens. We built the scanner to audit it, and we built the trail layer to produce it. We ran both on ourselves before showing either to anyone.
